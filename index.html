📖 Study Guide — Key Reference Notes

📜 Chapter 1: What Is a Notary Public?

  • Commissioned by the AZ Secretary of State
  • Commission term: 4 years (four years minus one day from effective date)
  • Commissioned in your county of residence, but can notarize statewide
  • Cannot notarize documents outside Arizona
  • Must be impartial — no personal or financial interest in the document

✅ Chapter 2: Eligibility Requirements (A.R.S. § 41-312)

  • At least 18 years old
  • Arizona resident
  • Able to read and write English
  • U.S. citizen OR legal permanent resident (green card)
  • No felony conviction (unless civil rights restored)
  • No conviction for offense involving moral turpitude
  • No AZ notary commission revoked within past 4 years

📋 Chapter 3: The 6 Notarial Acts in Arizona 2026 UPDATED

1. Acknowledgment
  • Signer appears before notary and acknowledges their signature
  • Signer MAY have signed before appearing (pre-signed allowed)
  • Notary verifies identity + willingness
  • Most common — used for deeds, powers of attorney, loan docs
2. Jurat
  • Signer MUST sign in front of notary
  • Signer takes an oath or affirmation
  • Used for affidavits and sworn statements
  • Key word in certificate: "subscribed and sworn"
3. Copy Certification
  • Notary certifies a copy is true and accurate
  • Cannot certify vital records (birth/death/marriage certificates)
  • Notary must personally make the copy
4. Oath / Affirmation
  • Oath invokes a higher power ("so help me God")
  • Affirmation is secular — same legal weight
  • Used in jurats and for administering oaths to witnesses

🔏 Chapter 4: Notary Seal Requirements

  • Rectangular: max 1.5" H × 2.5" W, OR Circular: max 1.5" diameter
  • Must contain: notary's name, "Notary Public," "State of Arizona," commission expiration date, commission number
  • Dark reproducible ink (black, dark blue, or purple)
  • Only ONE official seal per commission
  • Keep seal secure — report loss/theft within 10 days (A.R.S. § 41-328) — $1,000 fine if not reported!
  • New seal required upon every renewal (new expiration date)

📓 Chapter 5: The Notary Journal

  • Permanently bound journal — required by AZ law
  • Must record every notarial act in chronological order
  • Journal entries must include: date/time, type of act, document description, signer name, ID method, signer signature, fee charged
  • Retain journal for 5 years after last entry
  • Report loss/theft within 10 days
  • Surrender journal to SOS within 3 months after commission expires

⚠️ Chapter 6: Key Fees & Deadlines (TEST HEAVILY)

  • Max notary fee: $10.00 per notarial act (A.R.S. § 41-341)
  • Must post fee schedule in conspicuous place
  • Address change: notify SOS within 30 days — $25 penalty for late reporting
  • Seal/journal lost/stolen: notify SOS within 10 days — $1,000 fine for failure
  • After expiration: surrender seal + journal within 3 months
  • Commission term: 4 years
  • Renewal window: up to 60 days before expiration
  • Application fee: $43 to AZ Secretary of State
  • Bond: $5,000 surety, 4-year term, ~$25

🚫 Chapter 7: What a Notary CANNOT Do

  • Give legal advice or explain document contents to signers
  • Notarize their own documents or signature
  • Notarize for a spouse or immediate family member
  • Certify vital records (birth, death, marriage certificates)
  • Notarize documents with blank spaces
  • Perform a notarial act if signer appears impaired or coerced
  • Notarize outside Arizona
  • Use a notary seal after commission expires

📱 Chapter 8: Remote Online Notarization (RON)

  • Authorized in Arizona — A.R.S. § 41-351 et seq.
  • Must be authorized by SOS (no additional fee)
  • Requires a state-approved RON platform
  • Signer must be identified via knowledge-based authentication (KBA) or credential analysis
  • Both audio-visual recording AND journal entry required
  • Recordings kept minimum 5 years
  • Notary's state of commission determines RON authority — you notarize under AZ law regardless of signer location

🔑 Chapter 9: Signer Identification

  • Acceptable ID: current government-issued photo ID (driver's license, passport, state ID, military ID)
  • Must be satisfied the signer is who they claim to be
  • Personal knowledge also acceptable — you already know the signer
  • Credible witness: one person known to you who identifies the signer
  • Do NOT notarize if you cannot satisfactorily identify the signer

💲 Chapter 10: Fees, Liability & Ethics

  • Max fee: $10/act. Travel fee is separate and unregulated
  • Surety bond protects the PUBLIC — NOT the notary
  • If bond claim is paid, notary must reimburse the bonding company
  • Notary has unlimited personal liability — bond is NOT a liability cap
  • E&O insurance protects the notary personally
  • Notary is a "ministerial" officer — performs acts according to law, does not exercise judgment about wisdom of signers' decisions

🆕 August 2026 Manual — Key Changes & Clarifications

⚠️ CRITICAL CORRECTION: Arizona notaries may perform SIX notarial acts — not four. The 2025/2026 manual explicitly states 6 per A.R.S. § 41-251(6)(b). Acts 5 and 6 below are commonly missed on the exam.

ActDescriptionKey Rule
1. AcknowledgmentSigner acknowledges their signaturePre-signing OK · Signer must appear
2. JuratSigner swears/affirms document contents are trueMust sign IN PRESENCE · No blank spaces
3. Copy CertificationCertifies copy = true copy of originalNotary must make the copy · No public records
4. Oath/AffirmationSigner makes solemn declarationOath invokes deity · Affirmation is secular
5. Witnessing/Attesting a Signature ★ NEWNotary confirms person signed in their presenceNo oath · No acknowledgment · Signer must be present
6. Protest of Negotiable Instrument ★ NEW3rd-party witness to dishonoring a check/noteRare · Usually at financial institutions

Other key 2026 updates to know for the exam:

  • Exam fail rule: Fail 3 times = wait 1 YEAR (not just 30 days each time)
  • Copy cert excluded docs expanded: Marriage · Birth · Death · Divorce · Court Records · Real Estate Deeds
  • Fee is per SIGNATURE (not per document) · Max $10 per signature
  • Fee overcharging: 4x fee to aggrieved party + Class 5 felony
  • Notario Publico: Class 6 felony + permanent revocation
  • Travel fee: AZ state employee mileage rate (not unregulated) · Service/transaction fees PROHIBITED
  • Two journals required when performing both public AND non-public record notarizations
  • 6-month repeat rule: Same signer within 6 months — only need ID + signature once per period
  • Mobile ID app from the jurisdiction is now explicitly acceptable
  • Thumbprint or X mark is an acceptable signature — no witness needed
  • Replacement seal must have DIFFERENT shape AND ink color from stolen/lost seal
  • Application validity: Cannot be older than 90 days — must regenerate
  • Bond: Employer CANNOT cancel a departing employee's bond
  • Failure to surrender seal/journal: $50–$500 forfeiture (different from the $1,000 loss-report penalty)
  • Apostilles: AZ SOS ONLY — notaries have NO authority to issue apostilles

📋 How to Get Your AZ Notary Commission

⏱ Total Timeline: 6–8 weeks

Most of the wait is AZ SOS processing (4-6 weeks) — you can't speed that up. But you CAN get everything else done fast.

1
Download & Study the 2025 AZ Notary Manual
Get the official 2025 Notary Public Reference Manual at azsos.gov/business/notary-public. This is the ONLY source the exam pulls from. Read every page. This site covers the key material — use both.
⏱ Week 1💲 Free
2
Register for & Pass the Prometric Exam
Go to azsos.gov and click Notary Exam Registration. Pay $46.75. Schedule at one of 10 AZ testing centers or take remote. Exam: 45 questions, open-book (digital), 60 minutes, 80% to pass. Save your Prometric Candidate ID — required for your application.
⏱ Week 1-2💲 $46.75★ NEW July 2025

3
Purchase & Notarize Your $5,000 Surety Bond
Buy a 4-year $5,000 surety bond (~$25) from any licensed AZ insurance agent or notary vendor (arizonanotaries.com). Get it notarized by another commissioned AZ notary. The bond must have NO blank spaces when submitted.
⏱ Week 2💲 ~$25
4
Complete & Mail Your Application
Complete the application at azsos.gov. Print it — must be signed and mailed (not digital). Mail to: AZ Secretary of State, Business Services, 1700 W. Washington St., Floor 7, Phoenix AZ 85007-2808. Include: (1) signed application, (2) notarized bond (duplicate), (3) check/money order for $43 payable to "Arizona Secretary of State."
⏱ Week 2💲 $43No cash by mail
5
Wait for Commission Certificate (4-6 weeks)
The AZ SOS will review and mail your Commission Certificate. No expedited option available. Processing time is approximately 4-6 weeks. Check your mailbox. When it arrives, verify your name, county, and commission dates immediately.
⏱ Weeks 3-8No expedited available
6
Buy Your Seal & Journal
After receiving your certificate: purchase your official rubber notary seal and a permanently-bound notary journal. Your seal must match your commission exactly (name, county, commission number, expiration). Cannot perform any notarial act until you have BOTH the certificate and the seal.
⏱ Week 8💲 $35-70
7
Get E&O Insurance (Strongly Recommended)
The $5,000 bond protects the public — not you. Errors & Omissions insurance protects YOU if a client claims your mistake caused them financial harm. Available through NNA, Howden, or your insurance agent. 4-year term aligns with your commission.
⏱ Before 1st signing💲 ~$60-120/4yr
8
🎉 YOU ARE A COMMISSIONED AZ NOTARY!
You're legal. You're stamped. You're Compass Notary Services. Now go build something amazing. — Your commission is valid for 4 years. Renew up to 60 days before expiration. New renewal requires passing the Prometric exam again.
